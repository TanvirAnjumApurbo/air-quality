**Note on seasonal-naive.** At h = 24 the seasonal-naive rule $\hat y(t+h)=y(t+h-24)$
reduces to $y(t)$, which is persistence. The two rows are identical at that
horizon by definition, not by accident.

**Persistence error is not monotone in the horizon.** It peaks at h = 12 (98.65 µg/m³) and *falls* by h = 24 (73.76 µg/m³). This is the
diurnal cycle, not an error in the table: at h = 24 persistence compares a
time with the same clock hour one day earlier, whereas at intermediate
horizons it compares opposite phases of a strong daily cycle — morning peak
against afternoon minimum. Any skill score is therefore measured against a
reference whose difficulty varies with the horizon, which is precisely why
the raw RMSE column is reported alongside it.

### Is the selected architecture stable?

Tier 3 is ranked by mean validation loss across seeds, never by test
error. This validation split is thin, so the rule was re-run on single
seeds, on leave-one-seed-out subsets, and on 2,000 resamples of the
seeds, to see how often it returns the same winner.

| Horizon   | Selected        | Single seed   | Leave-one-out   | Seed bootstrap   | Distinct winners   | ρ(val, test)   | Regret (RMSE)   |
|:----------|:----------------|:--------------|:----------------|:-----------------|:-------------------|:---------------|:----------------|
| 1 h       | gru_h64_l2_w24  | 80%           | 100%            | 78%              | 2                  | +0.25          | +0.76           |
| 3 h       | gru_h64_l1_w24  | 40%           | 40%             | 49%              | 6                  | +0.62          | +0.62           |
| 6 h       | gru_h64_l1_w24  | 20%           | 60%             | 33%              | 5                  | +0.71          | +0.08           |
| 12 h      | gru_h64_l1_w168 | 0%            | 60%             | 47%              | 7                  | +0.40          | +1.57           |
| 24 h      | gru_h64_l1_w24  | 0%            | 60%             | 45%              | 10                 | +0.54          | +0.05           |

**The identity of the winner is not stable.** At 24 h the reported architecture wins 45% of seed resamples, 10 different candidates win at least once, and 6 of 36 sit inside the winner's own between-seed spread.

**The consequence is bounded.** Selecting on validation rather than on test costs +0.05 RMSE at this horizon (+0.08% of the best available), because the candidates it cannot separate are near-ties — the same conclusion the Model Confidence Set reaches in §1.

Validation loss remains informative here (Spearman ρ = +0.54 against the test ranking); it is simply not sharp enough to discriminate within the leading band.

Read `gru`/`lstm` results as *a* member of that band rather than as the uniquely correct architecture. The paper's claims rest on tier-level comparisons, which this does not disturb; a claim that one recurrent configuration beats another would not survive it.

Largest regret across horizons: +1.57 RMSE at 12 h (+2.69%).

   Intel RAPL counters, which is the normal case on Windows without elevated

## 6. AQI-category classification

Next-day (24 h) health-advisory classification into 6 AQI categories.

- Breakpoints: **us_epa_pm25_2024**, 2024 revision, effective 2024-05-06
- Source: US Environmental Protection Agency / AirNow, *Technical Assistance Document for the Reporting of Daily Air Quality -- the Air Quality Index (AQI), Table 6: Breakpoints for the AQI* — <https://document.airnow.gov/technical-assistance-document-for-the-reporting-of-daily-air-quailty.pdf>
- Bangladesh's Department of Environment states it applies the USEPA AQI equation
  and scale directly, so these are the correct breakpoints for a Bangladesh study.
- Target basis: **rolling_24h_mean** — the breakpoints are defined on a
  24-hour mean, so the label is the 24-hour mean ending at *t+h*, not the
  instantaneous hourly value.
- Class imbalance handled with `class_weight = balanced`.

- **Macro-F1 0.6215 ± 0.0057** over 5 seeds, across the 5 categories present in the test period
- Macro-F1 over all 6 defined categories: 0.5179 ± 0.0047
- Weighted-F1 0.6779, accuracy 0.6772, balanced accuracy 0.6281

Macro-F1 is the headline: the categories are severely imbalanced, so accuracy
alone would be dominated by the majority classes and would hide failure on the
hazardous categories an advisory exists to flag.

**Good** never occurs in the test period, so it has zero support. A category with no instances scores an F1 of zero whatever the model predicts, and averaging that in measures the test period's composition rather than the classifier. Both figures are given above; the present-class one is the model's performance.

The sequence tier ranks **1** on the more fragmented record (Dhaka, 82.3% coverage) and **3** on the near-complete one (Beijing, 98.9%).

**That ordering is the opposite of what a fragmentation account predicts**,
and it is reported here rather than set aside. Two cities differ in far
more than the continuity of their records: the comparison city's 24-hour
problem is simply harder, with persistence RMSE far above this city's, and
its test period is shorter and spans a seasonal transition. A two-city
contrast cannot separate fragmentation from any of that, which is why the
mechanism is tested by injection on a single record instead of inferred
from a pair. The controlled experiment and this observation disagree, and
the controlled experiment is the one with a valid counterfactual.

**Coverage is not the quantity that reaches the model.** Hourly
coverage runs Dhaka 82.3%, Beijing 98.9%, but forecast availability — the share of
test hours any model here can answer at all — runs
Dhaka 76.4%, Beijing Wanliu 72.5%, Beijing Dingling 64.4%, Beijing Dongsi 64.3%. A row needs an unbroken history behind it, so a scattered
outage costs far more than its own length, and a near-complete record
with many small gaps can be less available than a broken one with few
large ones. The fragmentation gradient this section reads its ordering
against is therefore much weaker than the coverage figures suggest,
and on these records it does not run the way coverage does. See the
availability section below.

**Strength of evidence.** How many models each city's own 95% Model
Confidence Set retains at this horizon — a set containing every candidate
means that city's ordering is not separable from noise:

- Dhaka: **5 of 9** — retains gru_h64_l1, ridge, random_forest, xgboost, lightgbm
- Beijing: **9 of 9** — no ordering is supported

Beijing cannot distinguish any method from any other here, persistence included. The ranking printed above for Beijing is therefore a description of this sample, not a finding, and no claim in this report rests on it.

Absolute skill is lower for every method in the comparison city: its test
period is shorter and spans a seasonal transition.

## 8. What a gap costs, and how often a model can answer

Every accuracy figure above is conditional on the model being able to produce a
forecast at all, and that condition has not so far been reported. A row is
scored only if it carries
`pos_in_run >= 168` hours of unbroken history and
a target 24 hours further on inside the same run, so a gap does not
cost the hours it removes: it costs those hours **plus the
192 behind it** that no longer reach back far enough.

The consequence is that the cost of missingness is governed by the *number* of
gaps rather than their total length. That is the gap-injection experiment's arm
contrast in closed form: at an identical hour count, scattering the removals
sterilises many radii where clustering them sterilises few — which is the
mechanism that experiment measures without explaining.

| Record | Missing hours | Missing (%) | Runs | Usable hours destroyed | Amplification |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Dhaka | 10,766 | 13.59 | 295 | 24,592 | 2.3x |
| Beijing Wanliu | 117 | 0.33 | 27 | 4,006 | 34.2x |
| Beijing Dingling | 424 | 1.21 | 43 | 5,953 | 14.0x |
| Beijing Dongsi | 509 | 1.45 | 39 | 5,314 | 10.4x |

**A near-complete record is not a well-supervised one.** Beijing Wanliu loses 34.2 usable hours for every hour missing, against Dhaka's 2.3, because amplification follows the number of runs and not the number of absent hours.

The relationship is close enough to state as a law. Writing $O$ for observed
hours, $k$ for distinct gaps and $R$ for the sterilisation radius,

$$\text{usable} \approx O\exp(\beta_0 - \alpha R k / O),\quad \alpha = 0.1555,\ \beta_0 = -0.1596$$

fitted on wanliu (101 cells) alone, it predicts 202 held-out cells from the other donor records at $R^2 = 0.977$ (median absolute error 5.2%).
 $\alpha$ is not a free constant: it should equal the share of gaps outliving the 3-hour forward-fill, which is 0.1418 — agreeing with the fitted value to 9%.

### Coverage is not what reaches the model

| Record | Lookback 168 h | 48 h | 24 h |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Dhaka | 76.4% | 87.2% | 89.4% |
| Beijing Wanliu | 72.5% | 85.5% | 88.7% |
| Beijing Dingling | 64.4% | 80.9% | 84.5% |
| Beijing Dongsi | 64.3% | 81.2% | 85.3% |

Forecast availability is the share of test hours a model can answer at all.
It does not rank these records the way coverage does: the primary record is
the least complete of the four and yet among the most available, because its
absences are clustered into a few long outages while the near-complete
stations' are scattered. **Coverage is what a data custodian reports;
availability is what a forecaster gets, and the two can order a set of
records in opposite directions.**

### Does a shorter reach cost accuracy?

Shortening the reach recovers those hours. It also removes real signal — the weekly lag and the 168-hour rolling statistics — so the trade is measured rather than assumed, on the 9,083 hours every arm can serve, which is the only comparison that is not confounded by how often each arm declines to answer.

**No capped arm differs from the status quo** on the common subset under Holm-corrected Diebold-Mariano across 16 tests.

The shorter reach is not better at forecasting. It is better at answering.

| Model | Status quo | Best arm | Availability | All-hours skill | Change |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `lightgbm` | +0.1765 | A24 | 97.6% | +0.1938 | +0.0173 |
| `random_forest` | +0.1827 | A24 | 97.6% | +0.1983 | +0.0156 |
| `ridge` | +0.1725 | A48 | 95.2% | +0.1791 | +0.0066 |
| `xgboost` | +0.1812 | A24 | 97.6% | +0.1966 | +0.0154 |

Scored over the whole evaluation universe, an hour with no forecast is not an
hour without error: it is an hour that must fall back on persistence. That is
where the recovered availability turns into recovered skill.

The three arms separate the two effects exactly. Holding the row set at the status quo and capping only the features isolates feature richness; holding the features and lowering the floor isolates supervision volume; in mean squared error the two sum to the total by construction (largest residual 0.00e+00).

| Model | Cap (h) | Total ΔMSE | Supervision volume | Feature richness |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `lightgbm` | 24 | -56.1 | -2.9 | -53.1 |
| `lightgbm` | 48 | -46.6 | +22.7 | -69.3 |
| `random_forest` | 24 | -29.6 | -17.0 | -12.6 |
| `random_forest` | 48 | -10.3 | +5.4 | -15.6 |
| `ridge` | 24 | +33.0 | +4.5 | +28.5 |
| `ridge` | 48 | +15.5 | -5.7 | +21.3 |
| `xgboost` | 24 | -30.5 | -10.3 | -20.2 |
| `xgboost` | 48 | -17.1 | +1.6 | -18.6 |

On these identical rows the larger term is **feature richness** (239 against 70 in summed absolute MSE). That is the right way round: the common subset holds the evaluation hours fixed, so extra training rows can only help through better-fitted parameters, and the availability they buy — which is where the gain of the previous table comes from — is by construction invisible here. Capping the reach removes the weekly lag and its rolling statistics and mostly *improves* the fit, so on this record the deepest features were paying for themselves only in the rows they made impossible.

**Scope.** One horizon and one record family. The frontier is measured at the
headline horizon only, and the arms cap the feature set rather than replacing it,
so this bounds what the configured reach costs — not what an optimally chosen
reach would buy. Below the sequence window the cap buys a recurrent model
nothing, because `max(lookback, window - 1)` is the binding floor.

## 9. Limitations

- **Energy figures are estimates.** See §5. They should not be reported as measurements.
- **One monitoring site.** The target series comes from a single monitor —
  US Embassy Dhaka (OpenAQ 2445+8415). Results describe that site, not Dhaka as a whole.
- **The record ends 2025-03-24.** No later data for this
  monitor exists in the archive the pipeline reads, so the test period cannot be
  extended without changing the data source.
- **Coverage.** 82.3% of hours observed, broken by
  2,074 distinct gaps; the longest runs 2,712 h
  (113 days, 2022-05-31 to 2022-09-21).
- **65,214 observed hours** after QC.
  5,056 negative readings were dropped rather than floored, which is a
  modelling choice, not a neutral act.
- **SARIMAX is fitted on a bounded training tail**, making it a recent-history
  statistical baseline rather than a full-sample one. Gaps inside that tail are
  left as NaN and handled by the Kalman filter rather than dropped, which would
  have changed the sampling interval the seasonal term depends on.
- **Ridge is structurally disadvantaged and should be read as a linear-model
  floor, not as a tuned competitor.** The target is modelled on a `log1p` scale
  while the PM2.5 history features are supplied on their raw scale, so a linear
  model has to express `log1p(y(t+h))` as a linear function of `y(t)`, which it
  cannot do well. Tree ensembles and the recurrent models are unaffected because
  they are non-linear in the inputs. This is a property of the feature/target
  parameterisation, not evidence that linear methods are hopeless on this task;
  supplying log-scale lag features would be the fair comparison and was not run.
- **Retransformation bias.** Predicting the conditional mean on a `log1p` scale
  and inverting with `expm1` returns something closer to a conditional median, so
  every model in this study will tend to under-predict the largest episodes. The
  bias applies identically to all models, so between-model comparisons remain
  valid, but absolute peak values should be read with it in mind.
- **Predictions are bounded** to the physical range QC applies to observations
  ([0, sanity cap]). Without the upper bound, unbounded linear extrapolation on
  the log scale produced a handful of impossible concentrations that dominated
  squared-error metrics. Clip counts are recorded per model in `results.json`.
