## 1. Headline

At the 24-hour horizon the best sequence model is **gru_h64_l1** (input window 24 h, 32,321 parameters, 5 seeds).

- RMSE **60.82 µg/m³** (persistence 73.76)
- MAE 37.84 µg/m³, R² 0.565, sMAPE 33.39%
- **Skill score vs persistence: +0.1754**

The best classical model at the same horizon is **random_forest**, RMSE 58.50 µg/m³, skill +0.2069.

**Note on seasonal-naive.** At h = 24 the seasonal-naive rule $\hat y(t+h)=y(t+h-24)$
reduces to $y(t)$, which is persistence. The two rows are identical at that
horizon by definition, not by accident.

**Persistence error is not monotone in the horizon.** It peaks at h = 12 (98.65 µg/m³) and *falls* by h = 24 (73.76 µg/m³). This is the
diurnal cycle, not an error in the table: at h = 24 persistence compares a
time with the same clock hour one day earlier, whereas at intermediate
horizons it compares opposite phases of a strong daily cycle — morning peak
against afternoon minimum. Any skill score is therefore measured against a
reference whose difficulty varies with the horizon, which is precisely why
the raw RMSE column is reported alongside it.

## 5. Green-AI accounting

### Two honesty requirements

1. **CodeCarbon reports estimates, not metered measurements.** It derives energy from
   hardware power models and falls back to fully modelled values when it cannot read
   Intel RAPL counters, which is the normal case on Windows without elevated
   privileges. Whether RAPL was readable is recorded per run in `results.json`.
2. **The Bangladesh figure is a recomputation, not a second measurement.** The same
   estimated kWh is multiplied by a cited national grid intensity:
   **696.13 gCO₂e/kWh (2025)**, Ember (2026), with major processing by Our World in Data, <https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/carbon-intensity-electricity>.

## 7. Limitations

- **Energy figures are estimates.** See §5. They should not be reported as measurements.
- **One monitoring site.** The target series comes from a single reference monitor
  (OpenAQ 2445 + 8415, the same physical instrument split across two provider records).
  Results describe that site, not Dhaka as a whole.
- **The record ends 2025-03-24.** The AirNow feed stops there; no Dhaka
  reference-monitor data exists after that date in the archive.
- **Coverage is fragmented.** 2,074 distinct gaps, of which a 113-day outage in 2022
  removes an entire monsoon season.
- **65,214 observed hours** after QC; 5,056 negative readings
  were dropped rather than floored, which is a modelling choice, not a neutral act.
- **SARIMAX is fitted on a bounded training tail**, making it a recent-history
  statistical baseline rather than a full-sample one. Gaps inside that tail are
  left as NaN and handled by the Kalman filter rather than dropped, which would
  have changed the sampling interval the seasonal term depends on.
- **Ridge is structurally disadvantaged and should be read as a linear-model
  floor, not as a tuned competitor.** The target is modelled on a `log1p` scale
  while the PM2.5 history features are supplied on their raw scale, so a linear
  model has to express `log1p(y(t+h))` as a linear function of `y(t)`, which it
  cannot do well. Tree ensembles and the recurrent models are unaffected because
  they are non-linear in the inputs. This is a property of the feature/target
  parameterisation, not evidence that linear methods are hopeless on this task;
  supplying log-scale lag features would be the fair comparison and was not run.
- **Retransformation bias.** Predicting the conditional mean on a `log1p` scale
  and inverting with `expm1` returns something closer to a conditional median, so
  every model in this study will tend to under-predict the largest episodes. The
  bias applies identically to all models, so between-model comparisons remain
  valid, but absolute peak values should be read with it in mind.
- **Predictions are bounded** to the physical range QC applies to observations
  ([0, sanity cap]). Without the upper bound, unbounded linear extrapolation on
  the log scale produced a handful of impossible concentrations that dominated
  squared-error metrics. Clip counts are recorded per model in `results.json`.
